Common questions

Does anything get uploaded?

No. Text recognition uses Apple's Vision framework, which runs on the iPhone itself. The app makes no network requests of any kind — not to a server of mine, because there isn't one, and not to anybody else's. The card image and the text read from it stay in Lanyard's own storage on your device. The only things that ever move are a file you export or a card you add to your own Contacts, and both happen because you asked.

Why does that matter?

Because of where business cards get scanned. If you are standing at a competitor's stand at a trade show, sending a photograph of the card you were just handed to a third-party server is a disclosure you probably did not intend to make — about the person who gave it to you, and about who you were talking to. Every mainstream card scanner requires an account and processes cards server-side. Lanyard is the one that cannot, because it has no server.

What is free, and what does the Full File add?

Ten scans, for the lifetime of the app — not ten a month. That is enough to point it at your own cards and see whether it reads them properly, which is the only question a free tier can honestly answer. Everything you scan in those ten is yours to keep, edit and export to Contacts.

The Full File removes the limit and adds batch mode, CSV and vCard export, event tagging, duplicate detection and search across every scan. It is $59.99 a year or $7.99 a month.

Why is there no free trial?

Deliberately. A trial that starts while you are at a conference and charges you a week later, at home, having forgotten about it, is a worse deal than being told the price up front. The ten scans do the same job without the ambush.

I deleted a card. Do I get the scan back?

No, and that is on purpose. The allowance counts scans, not stored cards, so deleting one does not return it. Otherwise the free tier would just be a game of delete-and-rescan, which would be unfair to everybody who paid and confusing for everybody who didn't.

How accurate is it?

On a clean, conventionally laid-out card, usually all of it. Lanyard tells you when it is not sure: fields it had to guess are highlighted in the review screen with the reason underneath — "read as the name because it is the largest text on the card and matches the email address" — so you can check its reasoning instead of taking the result on faith. Cards where the name is set over a photograph, or where the type is very small or very stylised, are the ones that need a correction.

How does it know which number is the mobile?

From the label beside it. Cards abbreviate — M, T, F, Mobil, Tél, Handy, Port. — and Lanyard knows those abbreviations in several languages. Where a number carries no dialling code, the country is taken from the address or the email domain so it still comes out dialable.

It got the first and last name the wrong way round.

Tap the field and swap them; it takes a second. Lanyard reads a family name printed first when the card signals it — a capitalised surname, a comma, an East Asian name — and it uses the email address to check itself, because soojin.kim@ settles an argument that typography cannot. When it has made that call without confirmation it flags the field rather than asserting it.

Can it scan a stack of cards at once?

Yes, with the Full File. Batch mode keeps the camera up: tap the shutter for each card and Lanyard queues and reads them in the background. Review the whole stack afterwards, on the plane home, and only the fields it doubted are waiting for you. The document scanner also takes several pages in one pass, which suits a pile of cards on a table.

Will the CSV import into my CRM?

That is the point of the format picker. The Salesforce, HubSpot, Outlook and Google Contacts layouts use the exact column names each of those importers matches on, so the file drops in without a field-mapping step. There is also a complete layout with every field Lanyard holds, and vCard 3.0 and 4.0 for everything else.

Does Lanyard read my address book?

No. It asks for permission to add contacts, never to read them. Duplicate checking runs against your own Lanyard scans, so there is no reason for the app to see your address book — and asking for the whole thing in order to save one card would be a bad trade.

What happens if I lose my phone?

Your cards are in an encrypted iPhone or iCloud backup if you make one, and nowhere else. There is no account to sign into on a new phone and no copy on a server to restore from. If that matters to you, export a CSV or vCard occasionally and keep it somewhere you control.

How do I cancel?

Subscriptions are managed by Apple, not by this app: open the Settings app, tap your name, then Subscriptions. Cancelling stops the next renewal and you keep the Full File until the period you have paid for ends.

The widget is not updating.

Remove it and add it again. If it still shows nothing, open the app once — the widget reads a small summary the app writes, and there is nothing to read until the app has run since installing. Names only appear on a widget with the Full File, and that is decided when the summary is written, so a free install never puts them there at all.
